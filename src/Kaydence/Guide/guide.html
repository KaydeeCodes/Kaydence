Kaydence Guide

A private diary, made by KaydeeCodes. Everything you need to know, in one place.

Welcome

Kaydence is a diary for your PC. It opens straight onto today, ready for you to write, and it keeps everything on your computer. Nothing is ever uploaded anywhere.

It works a bit like OneNote: you can click anywhere on the page and type, paste pictures and drag them wherever you like, and draw or highlight over the top. Alongside that there's a check-in panel for how you're feeling and anything else you want to keep track of, and a calendar that shows at a glance which days you wrote about and how they felt.

You never need to press save.Kaydence saves everything by itself a moment after you stop typing. You'll see "Saved" and the time at the bottom of the left sidebar.

The first time Kaydence opens, a short welcome tour helps you pick light or dark, a font, which check-in sections you want, whether you'd like the optional health, period and transition tools, and whether to use a password or a daily reminder. You can see it again from Settings, Help and feedback, Welcome tour.

You can open this guide any time from Settings, Help and feedback, or by pressing F1 anywhere in Kaydence.

Your first few minutes

  1. Just start typing. When Kaydence opens, the cursor is already waiting on today's page.
  2. Pick a face in the Feelings card on the right to say how your day is going. Click it again if you change your mind.
  3. Paste a picture with Ctrl+V, or drag one in from File Explorer. Drag it wherever you like.
  4. Open a section in the check-in panel, like Sleep or Tasks, if you want to note something there. Everything is optional.
  5. Look at the calendar on the left. Today now has a coloured dot, so you can see you wrote something.
TipHave a look through Settings once. You can choose light or dark, your favourite colour, lined or dotted pages, and hide any check-in sections you don't need.

The ribbon

The ribbon above your page works like the one in OneNote or Word. Click a tab name to switch what it shows.

TabWhat's on it

On the right of the tabs are Undo, Redo and Delete, which work whichever tab is showing.

Switching tabs puts the pen downGoing to Home or Insert switches back to Select and type, so a click on the page always starts writing. Your pen and shape choices are kept on the Draw tab for next time.

Writing on your page

Starting a text box

Click anywhere on an empty part of the page and start typing. Each click on empty space starts a new text box right where you clicked. You can also use Text box on the Insert tab. If a pen or shape is picked on the Draw tab, press Esc first to go back to Select and type.

If you click away from a text box without typing anything in it, it quietly disappears, so you never end up with empty boxes lying around.

Moving and resizing text

  • Hover over a text box and a small grab bar appears along its top. Drag the bar to move the box anywhere.
  • Drag the thin line on the right hand edge to make the box wider or narrower.
  • Right click the grab bar for Bring to front, Send to back and Delete this text.

Making your writing look how you want

Select some words, or just click in a line, then use the Home tab:

  • B, I and U for bold, italic and underline, or Ctrl+B, Ctrl+I and Ctrl+U.
  • The two A buttons make text bigger or smaller a step at a time.
  • Title and Heading make the whole line big and bold, no need to select it first. Normal puts it back.
  • Bullet points and numbered lists. Press Enter twice to finish a list.
  • Text colours change the colour of the words. The first dot puts them back to the normal colour, which changes with light and dark mode.
  • Highlight colours go behind the words, like a highlighter pen. The cross takes the highlight off.
  • Clear takes all the formatting off the words you've selected.

If you haven't clicked into any writing yet, the bottom of the sidebar says "Click into some writing first".

Adding the time

Time on the Insert tab types the current time where your cursor is, handy for noting when something happened.

Pasting text

Paste into a text box as normal with Ctrl+V. If you paste while no text box is selected, Kaydence makes a new box for it.

Spell check

Spelling mistakes get a red squiggle. Right click the word for suggestions. Spell check uses UK English and can be switched off in Settings, Writing.

Text size

Settings, Look and feel, Text size for new writing sets the size of new text boxes. Text you've already written keeps its size.

Sticky notes and checklists

Sticky notes

On the Insert tab, click one of the coloured dots next to Sticky note. A square note appears on your page, ready to type in. They work just like text boxes: drag the bar at the top to move them, drag the right edge to make them wider, and use all the Home tab formatting.

  • Right click the bar at the top and choose Sticky note colour to change its colour.
  • Sticky notes always use dark writing, so they're easy to read in dark mode too.
  • An empty sticky note stays on the page until you delete it, unlike an empty text box.

Checklists

Click Checklist on the Insert tab and type your first item.

To do thisDo this

Join a line onto the one above, or remove an empty linePress Backspace at the start of the line
Move between linesThe Up and Down arrows

Ticked lines are crossed out and faded. Checklists show up in Search and in printouts, with [x] for ticked lines.

Checklist or Tasks?The Tasks section in the check-in panel can carry unfinished tasks over to the next day. A checklist on the page stays on that day, like a list written in a notebook.

Voice notes

Sometimes it's easier to say it than type it. Click Voice note on the Insert tab and start talking.

  1. A small window opens with a red dot, a timer and bars that move with your voice. It records from your default microphone.
  2. Click Stop and save (or press Enter) when you're done. Cancel throws the recording away.
  3. The voice note appears on your page as a bubble, like in WhatsApp.

A voice note can be up to 5 minutes long. At 5:00 it stops and saves by itself.

Listening back

  • Click the round play button, or select the bubble and press Space, to play and pause.
  • Click anywhere on the waveform to jump to that part.
  • Drag the bar above the bubble to move it. Right click for Save a copy as a sound file, handy for sending to someone, plus Bring to front, Send to back and Delete.

Voice notes are saved in that day's folder as voice-...wav files, and they're encrypted with everything else when your password is on. Search and printouts show them as "(Voice note, 1:23)".

If Kaydence can't hear youWindows has a privacy switch for microphones. If the recorder says it can't hear anything or can't use the microphone, click Open Windows microphone settings in the recorder and make sure Microphone access and Let desktop apps access your microphone are both on. Also check the microphone isn't muted and is set as the default in Windows sound settings.

Pictures

Adding a picture

  • Paste it with Ctrl+V. This works with screenshots (Win+Shift+S), pictures copied from a website, and picture files copied in File Explorer. If you're typing when you paste, the picture lands just under what you're writing.
  • Drag it in from File Explorer and drop it where you want it.
  • Use Picture on the Insert tab to pick one or more files.

Big pictures are shrunk to a sensible size when they arrive. You can always make them bigger again.

Photos are cleaned when they arrivePhotos from phones and cameras often hide extra details inside the file, like the exact place the photo was taken, the date and the camera model. When you add a photo, Kaydence saves a clean copy without any of that, turned the right way up. Your original file is never changed.

Selecting, moving and resizing

With Select and type picked, click a picture to select it. A purple frame appears around it.

  • Drag the picture to move it anywhere.
  • Drag the round handle on the bottom right corner to resize it. It always keeps its shape.
  • Right click it for Bring to front, Send to back and Delete this picture.

Deleting a picture

Select it, then do any of these:

  • Click the little red bin that appears on its top right corner.
  • Press Delete or Backspace.
  • Click the bin button at the top right of the ribbon.

Changed your mind? Press Ctrl+Z and it comes straight back.

Rotating and cropping

Right click a picture, or select it and use the buttons on the Insert tab:

  • Rotate left and Rotate right turn it a quarter turn.
  • Crop... opens a window with your picture. Drag a box over the part you want to keep. Drag inside the box to move it, or click Keep it all to start again. Click Crop when you're happy.
  • Reset size puts the picture back to the size it was when you first added it.

Rotating and cropping save a new copy of the picture, so Ctrl+Z can still bring the original back.

Text and pictures together

Pictures float on the page. They never push your writing around, so you can put text next to them, on top of them or underneath them. Use Bring to front and Send to back to choose what sits on top.

Where pictures are storedEvery picture is copied into that day's folder inside your diary, so it's safe even if you delete the original.

Drawing and highlighting

All the drawing tools are on the Draw tab.

ToolWhat it does
Select and typeThe normal tool. Click to write, click pictures to select them.
PenDraw with the mouse, a touchpad or a pen. Pick a colour from the dots that appear next to it, and thin, medium or thick.
HighlighterA see-through marker for highlighting writing or pictures. Yellow, green, pink or blue.
EraserClick or drag over a line to rub the whole line out.
LassoDraw a loop around some of your drawings to select them. Then drag to move them, drag the corners to resize, or press Delete to remove them. The lasso only picks up drawings, not text or pictures.

Shapes

Pick a line, arrow, rectangle, circle or heart, then press and drag on the page. You'll see the shape as you drag, and it's drawn when you let go. Shapes use the pen colour and thickness.

  • Hold Shift while dragging for perfectly straight lines and arrows (in steps of 45 degrees), squares, perfect circles and even hearts.
  • Shapes are drawings like anything from the pen, so the eraser rubs them out, the lasso moves and resizes them, and undo works on them.

Drawings sit on a layer over everything else, so you can circle part of a photo or underline some writing. Press Esc to go back to Select and type.

Undo and redo

Press Ctrl+Z to undo and Ctrl+Y (or Ctrl+Shift+Z) to redo, or use the curved arrow buttons at the top right of the ribbon.

Undo works for everything on the page: typing, adding or deleting pictures, moving things, resizing and drawing. Each step is whatever you did before you paused, so a burst of typing undoes in one go.

  • Undo history belongs to the day you're on. When you move to another day, it starts fresh.
  • Undo in the check-in panel works inside each box with the normal Ctrl+Z.

Zooming the page

  • Hold Ctrl and roll the mouse wheel over the page. It zooms towards wherever your mouse is.
  • Ctrl+plus and Ctrl+minus zoom in and out, and Ctrl+0 goes back to 100%.
  • On the View tab, Fit page shrinks the page until everything on it fits in the window. Great for seeing a busy day all at once.

Zoom goes from 25% to 300%. It only changes how the page looks, never the size of anything saved, and it stays the same as you move between days.

Moving between days

  • Click any day on the calendar.
  • Use the arrows next to the date, or Alt+Left and Alt+Right.
  • Click Go to today, or press Ctrl+T from anywhere.
  • Click a day on the Week, Month, Memories or Search screens to open it.

If you leave Kaydence open overnight on today's page, it moves to the new day by itself the next time you come back to it.

The calendar

The small calendar on the left works like Outlook's.

  • A dot under a day means you wrote or checked in that day. Its colour matches the mood you picked. A grey dot means there's an entry but no mood.
  • A star means you marked that day as a milestone.
  • Today has a coloured ring, and the day you're looking at is filled in.
  • Scroll the mouse wheel over the calendar to flick through months.

Deleting a day

  1. Right click the day on the calendar.
  2. Choose Delete this day's entry.
  3. Click Yes to confirm.

The day isn't wiped. Its whole folder is moved into a deleted folder inside your diary, so you can bring it back if you need to. See Getting a deleted day back.

The check-in panel

The panel on the right is for quick, structured notes about your day. Every part is optional: fill in as much or as little as you like. Sections stay folded away until you click them. A section with something in it shows a small dot and a summary, like "7.5 h" or "118/76", so you can see what's filled in without opening it.

Hide the whole panel with the button on the right of the day header.

Choosing and arranging sections

  • Right click any section for Move to the top, Move up, Move down, Move to the bottom and Hide this section.
  • In Settings, Today panel, every section has a switch to show or hide it, and arrows to move it up or down. Kaydence remembers your order.
  • Hiding a section never deletes anything you wrote in it. Switch it back on and it's all still there.

Period and cycle

An optional period tracker. It's off unless you switch it on, either in the welcome tour or in Settings, Today panel, Period and cycle.

Logging

  • Open Period and cycle in the check-in panel and pick today's flow: Spotting, Light, Medium or Heavy. Click it again to clear it.
  • Tick anything you feel, like cramps, bloating, headache or tiredness. Pick as many as you like.
  • Add a note if you want to remember something else.

What Kaydence works out

  • The box at the top says which day of your cycle it is and roughly when your next period is due, or that it's late.
  • Days you logged a period get a small pink dot in the corner of the calendar. A pink ring marks days your next period might start.
  • The Week and Month screens show a pink Period label on logged days, and an outlined Period may start label on guessed days.
  • Insights shows your average cycle and period length, your next expected period, possible fertile days, and a bar for each recent cycle.
  • Printouts for your doctor include your flow and how you felt on the days you logged them.

Spotting is recorded but doesn't count as the start of a period. Guesses use your last six cycles, and a typical 28 day cycle until you've logged a few.

Only a rough guessPredictions are estimates from your own past cycles. They aren't medical advice and must never be used as contraception.

Days since counters

Under the calendar you can keep counters like "142 days since starting HRT" or "30 days alcohol free".

  1. Click the small + next to Days since in the sidebar.
  2. Type a name, or click one of the ideas like Started HRT or Smoke free.
  3. Type the date it started, like 14/3/2025, or click Today. Kaydence shows the full date underneath so you can check it's right.
  4. Click Save.
  • Hover over a counter to see the date and how long that is in years and months.
  • Click a counter to change it, or right click it for Start again from today and Delete this counter.
  • On round numbers like 7, 30, 50, 100 and 365 days, and on anniversaries, the counter says Milestone today.
  • A date in the future counts down instead, like "12 days to go" for an appointment or surgery.

Hide the counters in Settings, Look and feel, Show days since counters.

Week, Month, Memories and Search

Week

Seven days side by side, each with its mood face, the start of what you wrote, the first picture, period labels if you track your cycle, and a few facts like sleep and tasks. Use the arrows to move between weeks, and click any day to open it.

Month

The whole month in a big grid. Each day is tinted in its mood colour, with a snippet and a picture if it has one. On the right you'll find how your month felt, how many days you wrote, your longest writing streak, how many pictures and words, and a highlight of the month.

Memories

On this day shows what you wrote on today's date in past years. Your milestones lists every day you marked as a milestone, newest first, grouped by year.

Search

Press Ctrl+F or click Search, then type a word. Kaydence searches everything: your page, and every check-in box. Narrow it down by clicking mood faces, or tick Only milestone days. Results show newest first with your search word highlighted. Click one to open that day.

Printing and PDFs

Click Print on the icon rail, or press Ctrl+P. The preview on the right updates as you change things.

  1. Choose the days. Pick Last 7 days, This month, Last month, Last 3 months or Everything, or type your own dates in the two boxes like 01/09/2026.
  2. Choose who it's for. Just for you includes your writing, pictures and drawings. Your doctor leaves out your personal writing and pictures, and includes your check-in, medications, pain, blood pressure, weight, period and transition details instead.
  3. Fine tune what's included with the tick boxes: writing, pictures and drawings, mood, mood chart, health charts, check-in, health, and HRT or treatment. Health and transition options only appear if those sections are switched on.
  4. Choose the layout. Tick Start each day on a new page if you'd like every day on its own sheet.
  5. Click Print, or Save as PDF.

Saving a PDF

Save as PDF opens the print window with Microsoft Print to PDF already chosen. Click Print, then Windows asks where to save the file and what to call it.

For appointmentsChoose Your doctor and Last month, save a PDF, and either print it or email it. Every day has its own coloured band with the date, so nothing runs together.

Charts of sleep, weight, blood pressure and pain puts a chart for each one at the top, straight after the mood chart. Your doctor ticks it for you.

When health details or health charts are included, the printout starts with a Where it hurt body heatmap for the days you picked, with the worst areas listed beside it, and each day lists where it hurt and how badly.

Each day's details, like Medication, Sleep and Pain, are printed with bold headings so they're easy to scan. Drawings from your page are printed as a picture under that day's photos. Only days with an entry are printed.

Exporting your diary

Your words are yours to keep. You can copy your whole diary out of Kaydence at any time, into files that open on any computer without Kaydence.

Export to a folder

  1. Open Settings, Your diary folder and click Export to a folder.
  2. Choose where the export should go, like your Documents folder or a USB stick.
  3. Kaydence makes a new folder called Kaydence export with today's date, and opens it when it's done.

Inside you'll find:

  • Kaydence diary.html, one web page with every day, its mood, writing, pictures, voice notes, drawings and check-in. Open it in any web browser.
  • A folder for every day, sorted by year, with a text file of the writing and check-in, the pictures, the voice notes as normal sound files, and a picture of any drawings.
  • Read me.txt, explaining what everything is.

Save everything as a PDF

Click Save as PDF in the same place. Kaydence opens Print with Everything chosen, so you just press Save as PDF. See Printing and PDFs.

Exports are not encryptedEven if your diary has a password, an export is ordinary files that anyone with access to the folder can read. Kaydence reminds you of this before it starts. Keep exports somewhere private, and delete them when you no longer need them.

Your privacy

Kaydence was made to be private from the ground up.

  • Everything stays on your PC. Your diary, pictures, voice notes, settings and backups are stored in folders on your computer. There's no account, no sign in and no cloud.
  • No tracking. Kaydence has no analytics, no adverts and no usage statistics. It never counts what you do or sends it anywhere.
  • The only thing that goes online is the optional update check. Once a day Kaydence asks GitHub for the latest version number. Nothing about you or your diary is sent. You can switch it off in Settings, Help and feedback.
  • Links you click, like Report a problem or the website, open in your own web browser. Kaydence doesn't add anything from your diary to them.
  • Logs and bug reports never contain anything you wrote. See Logs.
  • Photos are cleaned of hidden location and camera details when you add them.
  • Encryption is optional and strong. See Password, lock and encryption.

The same privacy statement is on the website at kaydence.kaydee.codes/privacy.

Password, lock and encryption

A password is optional and switched off to begin with. When it's on, Kaydence asks for it before showing your diary, and every file in your diary is encrypted with it: your pages, pictures, voice notes, check-ins, counters and medications.

Setting a password

  1. Open Settings and go to Privacy and lock.
  2. Switch on Lock with a password.
  3. Type your password twice and click Save. It needs at least 4 characters.
  4. Kaydence encrypts your whole diary. A small window shows the progress, it only takes a moment.
  5. Choose where to save your recovery file. Please do. It's the only way back in if you ever forget your password.
  6. That's it. Kaydence also makes a fresh locked backup. If you have older backups with diary days in them from before the password, it asks whether to delete them, because anyone using the PC could still read those.

When it asks for your password

SettingWhat happens
Every time Kaydence opensYou're asked every time you start Kaydence.
Only if you haven't used it for a whileYou're only asked if you haven't used Kaydence for the number of days you choose in A while means this many days. This box only appears when this option is picked. So that Kaydence can open your encrypted diary without asking, Windows keeps a protected copy of the key that only your Windows account on this PC can use.
Lock when you step awayLocks after a number of minutes without you touching Kaydence, even if it's still open.
Lock when it's hidden by the clockLocks as soon as Kaydence tucks itself away into the notification area.

You can lock straight away at any time with the Lock button on the icon rail, Ctrl+L, or Lock now on the clock icon's right click menu.

Changing or removing your password

Click Change next to Change your password, type your current password, then choose a new one. Your recovery file keeps working, and your diary doesn't need encrypting again. To remove the password, switch off Lock with a password and type your current password to confirm. Kaydence then decrypts your diary back into normal files.

What encryption protectsYour diary files are locked with AES-256 encryption. Someone who copies your diary folder, takes your hard drive or opens the files in File Explorer sees only scrambled data. Only your password or your recovery file can unlock them.
Please keep a recovery fileBecause your diary is really encrypted, there's no back door. If you forget your password and don't have a recovery file, nobody can get your diary back, not even the person who made Kaydence.

When your diary is encrypted and set to ask every time, Kaydence asks for your password in a small window before it opens. If it starts with Windows and is set to start hidden, it waits quietly as an icon by the clock instead, and only asks for your password when you click the icon or press your shortcut. While Kaydence is running, locking it with Ctrl+L hides everything until you type your password again.

Recovery file

A recovery file is the spare key for your encrypted diary. If you forget your password, it's the only way to open your diary and choose a new password.

Saving one

Kaydence asks you to save one as soon as you set a password. You can also make one any time in Settings, Privacy and lock, Recovery file, then Make a new one. Pick where to save it and you're done. The file ends in .kaydencekey.

Where to keep it

  • A USB stick you keep somewhere safe is ideal.
  • A second copy somewhere private, like a folder on another computer, also works.
  • Don't keep your only copy on the same PC. If the PC breaks, you want the recovery file somewhere else.
Treat it like a keyAnyone who has your recovery file and a copy of your diary or a backup can open it. Keep it private, and never keep it in the same place as your backups.

Making a new one

Making a new recovery file stops the old one working. Do this if you think someone else might have your old one. The settings page shows the date your current recovery file was made. Switching encryption on for the first time also needs a new recovery file, because older ones can't open an encrypted diary.

If you forget your password

With your recovery file

  1. On the lock screen, click Forgot your password? Use your recovery file.
  2. Find and choose your .kaydencekey file.
  3. Choose a new password and type it twice.
  4. Kaydence unlocks. Your recovery file still works for next time.

If Kaydence says the file doesn't match, it's probably an older recovery file from before you made a new one. Try any newer copy you have.

On the lock screen, or in the small window that asks for your password when Kaydence starts, it works the same way.

Without a recovery file

If your diary is encrypted, a forgotten password without a recovery file can't be worked around. That's what keeps your diary safe from everyone else too. Try any passwords you might have used, and check for copies of your recovery file on USB sticks or other computers.

If you set a password but chose not to encrypt when Kaydence asked, your files aren't encrypted, so you can still switch the password off by hand: close Kaydence completely, open %LocalAppData%\Kaydence\settings.json in Notepad, change "PasswordEnabled": true to false, save, and open Kaydence again.

Backups

Kaydence makes a zip copy of your whole diary once a day, the first time you open it that day. It keeps them for as long as you choose in Settings, then tidies older ones away.

SettingWhat it does
Back up automatically every dayMakes the daily copy. Leave this on.
Keep backups for7, 14, 30 or 90 days.
Backup folderWhere the copies go. Click Change to pick another folder, like a USB stick or a second drive, and Open to look inside.
Restore from a backupPuts your diary back to how it was in a backup. See Restoring from a backup.
Back up nowMakes an extra copy straight away, with the time in its name.

What the backup files are called

  • Kaydence-2026-09-30.zip is the automatic daily backup.
  • Kaydence-2026-09-30-181502.zip is one you made with Back up now.
  • Kaydence-before-restore-2026-09-30-181502.zip is made automatically just before a restore, holding your diary as it was.
Two places are safer than oneBy default backups sit on the same PC as your diary. That protects you from mistakes, but not from the PC breaking. Now and then, copy your backups folder to a USB stick, or set the backup folder to a second drive.

If the backup folder is on a USB stick that isn't plugged in, that day's backup is skipped and Kaydence tries again the next time you open it.

Restoring from a backup

The easy way, inside Kaydence

  1. Open Settings and go to Backups.
  2. Click Restore... next to Restore from a backup.
  3. Choose the backup zip you want. The date is in its name.
  4. Read the message and click Yes.
  5. Kaydence zips up your diary as it is right now into your backups folder, puts the backup in its place, and restarts itself.
Restoring an encrypted backupA backup is opened with the password, or recovery file, you had when that backup was made. If you've changed your password since, Kaydence will ask for the old one after restarting.
You can always undo a restoreBecause Kaydence saves a Kaydence-before-restore zip first, restoring never loses anything. If you picked the wrong backup, just restore that before-restore zip.

By hand, if Kaydence won't open

  1. Make sure Kaydence is closed, including by the clock.
  2. Press Win+R, type %LocalAppData%\Kaydence and press Enter.
  3. Rename the Diary folder to something like Diary old. Don't delete it yet.
  4. Open the Backups folder, right click the backup you want and choose Extract All.
  5. Extract it to a new folder called Diary inside %LocalAppData%\Kaydence. Inside it you should see an entries folder and an index.json file straight away, not another folder first.
  6. Open Kaydence and check everything looks right. Once you're happy, you can delete Diary old.

Getting back just one day

  1. Close Kaydence completely.
  2. Open the backup zip by double clicking it, then go into entries, the year, and find the day's folder, like 2026-09-30.
  3. Copy that folder into the same place in your diary: %LocalAppData%\Kaydence\Diary\entries\2026. Replace the existing one if Windows asks.
  4. Delete index.json in the Diary folder. Kaydence rebuilds it by itself next time it opens, so the calendar dots are right.
  5. Open Kaydence.

Getting a deleted day back

When you delete a day from the calendar, it's moved to Diary\deleted with a name like 2026-09-30 deleted 2026-10-01 101500. To bring it back:

  1. Close Kaydence completely.
  2. Open your diary folder (Settings, Your diary folder, or the folder button at the bottom of the sidebar), then open deleted. Settings also has a button that opens the deleted folder directly.
  3. Rename the day's folder back to just the date, like 2026-09-30.
  4. Move it into Diary\entries\2026 (use the right year).
  5. Delete index.json in the Diary folder so the calendar is rebuilt.
  6. Open Kaydence. The day is back with its dot on the calendar.

Where everything is kept

Everything lives in %LocalAppData%\Kaydence, which is usually C:\Users\YourName\AppData\Local\Kaydence. This folder is only on your PC and isn't synced to OneDrive. To get there quickly, press Win+R, type %LocalAppData%\Kaydence and press Enter.

File or folderWhat's in it
Diary\entries\2026\2026-09-30\One folder per day. day.json holds your writing and check-in, ink.isf holds drawings, the img-... files are your pictures and the voice-... files are your voice notes. When your password is on, they're all encrypted.
Diary\index.jsonA small summary of every day for the calendar dots. Safe to delete, it's rebuilt automatically.

Diary\keys.jsonOnly when your password is on. The lock for your encrypted diary, holding the diary key wrapped by your password and your recovery file. Never delete it, your diary can't be opened without it.

Kaydence always writes a temporary file first and then swaps it in, so a crash or power cut can never leave a half written day. If a day's file is ever damaged, Kaydence keeps a copy called day.json.broken-... before carrying on.

Moving to a new PC

  1. On the old PC, open Settings, Backups and click Back up now.
  2. Copy the new backup zip to a USB stick. If your diary is encrypted, you'll need your password or recovery file on the new PC too.
  3. Install Kaydence on the new PC and open it.
  4. Go to Settings, Backups, click Restore... and choose the zip from your USB stick.
  5. Kaydence restarts. If the backup is encrypted, it asks for your password, then opens your whole diary. Set up your other settings again on the new PC, as settings aren't part of the diary backup.

Kaydence can pop up a little Windows notification to remind you to write.

Updates, problems and ideas

Updates

Once a day, Kaydence asks GitHub for the number of the latest version. If there's a newer one, it tells you what's new and offers to open the download page. No skips that version, Cancel asks again another day.

Nothing from your diary is ever sentThe only thing Kaydence asks is "what's the latest version?". Switch it off in Settings, Help and feedback, Check for updates automatically, and use Check now whenever you like instead.

Reporting a problem or suggesting a feature

In Settings, Help and feedback, Report a problem and Suggest a feature open a new GitHub issue in your web browser, with your Kaydence and Windows version already filled in. You'll need a free GitHub account to send it. Describe what happened, and please attach a bug report (see below) so the problem can be found quickly.

Making a bug report

  1. Open Settings, Help and feedback and click Make a bug report.
  2. Choose where to save it. Kaydence suggests your desktop.
  3. Kaydence saves a zip file and shows it in File Explorer, then offers to open a new problem report on GitHub.
  4. Drag the zip file into the problem report.

The zip holds the last three days of logs, your Kaydence and Windows versions, how much space your diary uses, and a list of your settings. Passwords, keys and hashes are only ever shown as "set" or "none". Nothing you wrote is included. You can open the zip and read every file before sending it.

Logs

Kaydence keeps a log of what it does, like a black box recorder. If something goes wrong, the log shows exactly where.

  • Open it from Settings, Help and feedback, Logs, Open log folder. There's one file per day, like kaydence-2026-09-30.log, kept for 14 days.
  • Each time Kaydence starts, the log notes the app version, your Windows and .NET versions, screen size and similar details about your PC.
  • After that, every line has the date and time to the millisecond, what part of Kaydence it's about, and what happened: which day was opened, how long saving took, whether a picture loaded, which settings changed, and any errors in full.
Your diary never goes in the logThe log never contains what you wrote, your check-in answers, your password, your recovery key, what you searched for, or anything you copied. Your Windows user name is hidden in file paths. You're welcome to open a log in Notepad and check before sending it.

When you report a problem, the easiest way to share your logs is Make a bug report, which zips up the last few days for you.

Health features are not medical advice

Kaydence is a diary, not a medical app or medical device. The medication, symptoms, blood pressure, weight, transition and period tools are there so you can keep your own notes and share them with your doctor if you want to.

  • Charts, averages and summaries only show what you typed in. They don't diagnose anything or tell you what to do.
  • Period and cycle guesses are rough estimates from your own past cycles. They must never be used as contraception or to plan or avoid a pregnancy.
  • Kaydence doesn't remind you to take medication, check doses or warn about interactions.
  • Always talk to a doctor, nurse or pharmacist about your health, and in an emergency call your local emergency number.

Don't want any of the health tools? Switch off Health, Period and cycle and Transition in the welcome tour, or the individual sections in Settings, Today panel, and they disappear from the check-in panel and the print options.

Installing and uninstalling

Kaydence comes as a setup file called something like Kaydence-Setup-1.0.1.exe, where the number is the version. Run it and click through. It installs just for you, in %LocalAppData%\Programs\Kaydence, so it never asks for an administrator password.

  • It adds Kaydence to the Start menu, and a desktop shortcut if you tick that box.
  • Everything Kaydence needs is inside, there's nothing else to install.
  • To update, run the newer setup file. It closes Kaydence if it's open, updates it, and keeps all your settings.

Uninstalling

Open Windows Settings, Apps, Installed apps, find Kaydence and choose Uninstall. It removes the app and its start with Windows entry.

Your diary is never removedUninstalling leaves your diary, settings and backups in %LocalAppData%\Kaydence. Install Kaydence again and everything is exactly where you left it. To remove your diary too, delete that folder yourself after uninstalling.

Troubleshooting

I clicked on the page but nothing happens

You probably have a pen, eraser or shape picked on the Draw tab, so clicks draw instead. Press Esc, or click the Home tab.

I can't select a picture

Again, switch to Select and type. The lasso tool only selects drawings.

A picture shows "Picture not found"

Its file is missing from the day's folder. Restore just that day from a backup, see Getting back just one day.

The page looks tiny or huge

You've zoomed. Press Ctrl+0 to go back to 100%.

I don't get my reminder

Kaydence has to be running at the time, even if it's only by the clock. Also check that notifications are allowed in Windows Settings, System, Notifications, and that Do not disturb or Focus is off.

My voice note is silent

See If Kaydence can't hear you. Record a quick test and watch the bars move while you talk.

Kaydence asks for an old password after a restore

The backup was made when you had a different password. Type that one, or use the recovery file you had then. Once it opens, change the password in Settings if you like.

The calendar dots look wrong

Close Kaydence, delete Diary\index.json and open Kaydence again. It rebuilds the summary from your day files.

Kaydence says it couldn't save

Usually the disk is full or another program has the file open. Your writing is still on screen. Free up some space or close other programs, then type a letter and delete it to trigger another save.

I closed Kaydence but it's still running

You've switched on Closing the window keeps Kaydence running. Right click the icon by the clock and choose Exit Kaydence.

Kaydence doesn't start with Windows

Switch Start Kaydence when you sign in to Windows off and on again from the copy of Kaydence you normally use, so Windows remembers the right one. Also check Task Manager, Startup apps, to make sure Kaydence isn't disabled there.

I want to start completely fresh

Back up first! Then close Kaydence and rename %LocalAppData%\Kaydence to something else. Kaydence starts as new next time. Rename it back to return to your old diary.

Something went wrong

Kaydence tries to keep running and shows a message. The full details are in today's log, see Logs.
